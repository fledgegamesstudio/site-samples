When to wash a house in the Upstate

Pollen, humidity and red clay decide the best weeks to wash siding, decks and driveways here. A short guide to timing it right.

Oct 2026, 1 min read. By the Upstate Clean Co. crew.

Most Upstate houses need a wash once a year. The trick is picking the right weeks, because the weather here puts grime back on fast if you wash too early.

Wait until the pollen is done

Pine and oak pollen fall heavily in spring and coat everything in a yellow-green film. If you wash during the heaviest weeks, the siding is yellow again within days. Wait until the cars in your street stop turning yellow overnight, then book.

Shady walls need it most

Mildew grows where the sun does not reach: north walls, the side of the house under trees, and decks that stay damp. Those spots are often green while the sunny side still looks fine. Ask for them to be checked even if the rest of the house looks clean.

Siding gets a soft wash, concrete gets pressure

  • Siding and roofs: a low-pressure soft wash with a cleaning solution. High pressure can force water behind vinyl siding and strip paint.
  • Driveways and walks: a surface cleaner, which spins under a cover and leaves an even finish without stripes.
  • Decks: low pressure and the right tip, so the wood does not get fuzzy or etched.

Before the crew comes

  1. Close every window and check that outdoor outlets have covers.
  2. Move cars, grills, planters and toys off the area.
  3. Tell the crew about plants you care about, so they can wet and rinse them.

After red clay storms

Summer storms splash red clay up the bottom of siding and garage doors. It rinses off easily when it is fresh and stains when it sits, so a quick rinse after a big storm saves a harder clean later.
